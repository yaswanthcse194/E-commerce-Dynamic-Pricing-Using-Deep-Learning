Dynamic Pricing System Using Deep Learning

🎯 WHAT THIS SYSTEM DOES:
This Dynamic Pricing System uses Machine Learning (Random Forest) + Reinforcement 
Learning (Q-Learning) to recommend optimal product prices that maximize profit.

================================================================================
🔍 KEY TERMS EXPLAINED:
================================================================================

1. CURRENT PRICE (unit_price)
   - What it is: The actual/current selling price of the product in your dataset
   - Example: If unit_price = 50, your product is currently being sold at ₹50
   - Where you see it: In the dataframe column 'unit_price'

2. PREDICTED PRICE (predicted_price)
   - What it is: The price that the Random Forest model predicts based on:
     * Current demand (qty)
     * Competitor prices (comp_1, comp_2, comp_3)
     * Seasonality (month, quarter, year)
     * Historical price patterns
     * Price elasticity
   
   - How it's calculated: 
     predicted_price = RandomForestModel.predict([features])
   
   - Example: If predicted_price = 55, the model suggests the optimal price 
     should be ₹55
   - Purpose: Shows what price the market conditions suggest

3. PRICE CHANGE % (price_change_%)
   - What it is: Percentage difference between predicted price and current price
   - Formula:
     price_change_% = ((predicted_price - unit_price) / unit_price) × 100
   
   - Interpretation:
     * Positive % (e.g., +10%) → Predicted price is HIGHER → Consider increasing price
     * Negative % (e.g., -5%) → Predicted price is LOWER → Consider decreasing price
     * Near 0% (e.g., ±5%) → Current price is optimal → Keep same price

4. RECOMMENDATION
   - What it is: Action suggestion based on price change percentage
   - Logic:
     * If price_change_% > 5% → "Increase Price"
     * If price_change_% < -5% → "Decrease Price"
     * Otherwise → "Keep Same Price"

5. OPTIMAL PRICE (from Q-Learning)
   - What it is: The price that maximizes long-term profit using Reinforcement 
     Learning
   - How it's calculated:
     1. Q-Learning agent explores different prices (states)
     2. For each price, it calculates expected reward (profit)
     3. Q-value = Expected cumulative future profit
     4. Optimal price = Price with highest Q-value
   
   - Example Output:
     Optimal Recommended Price: 51
     Q-Value at this Price: 134841.20
   
   This means: ₹51 is the price that gives maximum expected profit 
   (Q-value = 134841)

6. PREDICTED DEMAND
   - What it is: Estimated quantity of products that will be sold at a given price
   - How it's calculated:
     demand = max(1, 100 - (current_price - predicted_price) × 3)
     * If price is too high → demand decreases
     * If price matches predicted → demand is stable
   - Example: If predicted_demand = 85, expect to sell ~85 units

7. ESTIMATED PROFIT
   - What it is: Total revenue expected at a given price
   - Formula:
     Estimated Profit = Price × Predicted Demand
   
   - Example Calculation:
     * Price = ₹51
     * Predicted Demand = 85 units
     * Estimated Profit = ₹51 × 85 = ₹4,335
   
   - Note: This is revenue, not net profit (doesn't subtract costs)

🎓 UNDERSTANDING THE TWO MODELS:
================================================================================

1. Random Forest Model (Supervised Learning)
   - Purpose: Predicts what price the market suggests based on historical data
   - Output: predicted_price
   - Use: Short-term price recommendations

2. Q-Learning Model (Reinforcement Learning)
   - Purpose: Finds the price that maximizes long-term profit
   - Output: optimal_price with highest Q-value
   - Use: Strategic pricing decisions

Why Both?
---------
- Random Forest: "What does the market say the price should be?"
- Q-Learning: "What price maximizes my profit over time?"
